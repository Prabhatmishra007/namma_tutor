1 · Ohm's Law

Set the voltage and resistance. The circuit, the current reading and the V–I line all update together.

Ohm's law: V = IR, provided temperature and physical conditions stay constant. The V–I graph of a resistor obeying Ohm's law is always a straight line through the origin — its slope is the resistance.

2 · Series vs Parallel

Same two resistors, same 12 V supply — two very different outcomes. Move R1 and R2 and compare the equivalent resistance on each side.

Series

R_eq = R1 + R2 + … Current is the same through every resistor; voltage splits.

Parallel

1/R_eq = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + … Voltage is the same across every resistor; current splits.

3 · Resistivity — the shape of a wire matters

R = ρL/A. Stretch the wire longer or thinner and watch resistance respond — this is exactly why thin, long heating elements get hot.

Where this shows up outside the exam

Series wiring

  • Old-style decorative light strings — one bulb fails, the whole string goes dark
  • Ammeters are placed in series so all the current passes through them
  • Fuses are wired in series so they can break the whole circuit

Parallel wiring

  • Household wiring — every appliance gets the full 220 V independently
  • Voltmeters are placed in parallel so they don't disturb the current
  • One appliance failing doesn't cut power to the rest of the house
