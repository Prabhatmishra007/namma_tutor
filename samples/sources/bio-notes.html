1 What is a Life Process?

the maintenance functions every living thing must keep running

Definition

Life processes are the basic functions performed by organisms to maintain their life — even when the body is not doing anything else visible. A resting person is still nourishing cells, exchanging gases, pumping blood and removing waste.

Nutrition Respiration Transportation Excretion
✎ Why "maintenance" is the key word

Growth and movement can pause — a seed can lie dormant for years. But nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion cannot stop even for a minute without damaging the organism. That is what separates a life process from an ordinary activity.

Board trap

Photosynthesis has TWO stages: the light reaction (splits water, releases O2, needs light directly) and the dark reaction (fixes CO2 into glucose, does NOT need light directly — it needs the products of the light reaction). Examiners love asking which stage a given event belongs to.

Remember

Amoeba has no fixed mouth. Any part of its cell surface can form pseudopodia ("false feet") to surround and trap food — this is exactly what your white blood cells do to bacteria in your own body.

✎ Saprophyte or parasite — the one-question test

Ask: is the source of food alive? A saprophyte feeds on dead, decaying matter — the organism it feeds on is already dead. A parasite feeds on a living host, and usually stays attached to it for its whole feeding life. Both are heterotrophic, but only one harms a living organism directly.

One-line recall

"Saprophytes clean up the dead. Parasites drain the living."

5 Nutrition in Human Beings

Fig 1.4 — the human alimentary canal (simplified)
✎ What each gland adds

PancreasEnzymes for all 3 food groups
✎ Stomach's own job

Gastric glands release HCl (kills germs, activates pepsin) and pepsin (begins protein digestion). Mucus lines the wall so the stomach doesn't digest itself.

Exam-line worth memorising

"Most digestion AND most absorption both finish in the small intestine — that's why it's the longest part of the canal."
