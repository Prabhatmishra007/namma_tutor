1 · The Law of Reflection

Move the slider — watch the angle of reflection copy the angle of incidence, exactly, every time.

∠i = 40° ∠r = 40° Both rays lie in the same plane as the normal
First law

The incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.

Second law

The angle of incidence always equals the angle of reflection — both measured from the normal, never from the mirror surface.

2 · Concave Mirror — Ray Diagram

Slide the object further from or closer to the mirror. Watch the image flip from real to virtual as the object crosses the focus F.

Quick recall — five zones, five images. Beyond C: real, inverted, diminished. At C: real, inverted, same size. Between C & F: real, inverted, magnified. At F: image at infinity. Between F & P: virtual, erect, magnified.

Where each mirror is actually used

Concave mirror

  • Shaving / makeup mirrors — object held within F, gives a magnified erect image
  • Torches, headlights, solar cookers — bulb placed at F sends out a parallel beam
  • Dentist's mirror — small concave mirror held close to a tooth

Convex mirror

  • Vehicle rear-view mirrors — diminished image means a wider field of view
  • Security mirrors in shops — one mirror covers a whole aisle
  • Street-light reflectors — spreads light over a larger area
